What changed when the rules changed?

A causal look at prescribing in Scotland: not just what happened, but what would have happened otherwise.

Scroll

A steady climb

For four years, the number of items dispensed each month rose steadily, with a familiar winter bump every year.

Then the rules changed

In April 2019 pregabalin and gabapentin were reclassified as controlled drugs, with stricter prescribing and dispensing rules.

The road not taken

To judge the change, we need a comparison: what prescribing would have looked like if nothing had happened.

We fit the trend and seasonality from the years before, then project it forward. The shaded band is the range we would expect from chance alone.

What actually happened

Prescribing dropped below that expected path almost immediately, and the gap kept widening.

Measuring the gap

The shaded area is the estimated effect of the change: the distance between what happened and what we expected.

Not the same everywhere

Repeating the analysis for each deprivation quintile shows whether the change reached every community equally.

When is this causal?

The estimate only holds if:

  • nothing else changed at the same moment;
  • the old trend would have continued;
  • recording stayed consistent.

A control series, such as a similar medicine that was not reclassified, tests the first assumption. If it shows no break, the case gets stronger.

Source: simulated data for layout testing. Final version: Public Health Scotland, Scottish Health and Social Care Open Data.

About this piece

Method. Interrupted time series. A linear trend plus yearly seasonality is fitted by least squares to the months before the change, then projected forward with 95% prediction intervals. The effect is the difference between observed and projected values.

Data. Every number on this page is currently simulated. The final version will use Public Health Scotland's Prescriptions in the Community open data.

Author. Juliet Asantewaa Sarpong, PhD student in statistics (causal inference), University of Edinburgh.
